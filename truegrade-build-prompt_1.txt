Build a multi-tenant heavy civil / sitework construction estimating web app 
called TrueGrade.

END-TO-END WORKFLOW (the whole product exists to deliver this):
1. A company sets up its own costs once (labor, equipment, materials, 
   suppliers, production rates) and can refine them anytime.
2. For each bid, an estimator uploads the plan set and bid documents issued 
   by the owner (federal, state DOT, county/city, or private developer).
3. TrueGrade reads the documents, extracts quantities, and builds a draft 
   quantity takeoff. The estimator reviews and corrects it against the 
   plan sheets.
4. Confirmed quantities flow into the estimate, which calculates labor, 
   equipment, and materials from the company's own data.
5. TrueGrade generates a consolidated material list and creates 
   supplier-ready RFQ spreadsheets, grouped by supplier category.
6. The estimator either sends RFQs from inside the app OR downloads them and 
   sends them from their own email to anyone they choose. Either way, 
   supplier prices come back (filled spreadsheet, online form, or the 
   supplier's own PDF quote) and populate the estimate after review.
7. The app produces the final bid documents. After the job, actual costs 
   feed back to calibrate future estimates.

BRANDING (single source of truth):
- Define PRODUCT_NAME = "TrueGrade" in ONE config file. Use it for every page 
  title, header, email, PDF footer, spreadsheet header, and UI string. Never 
  hardcode the name anywhere else.
- Tagline (config value): "Estimates calibrated to your real costs."
- Initial deployment: interstaterock.theanswerai.com (tenant #1: Interstate 
  Rock). Architect so additional contractors can be added as separate 
  tenants on their own subdomains with no schema changes.
- White-label: each company sets its own logo and accent color. On estimate 
  PDFs and RFQ spreadsheets, their branding leads and "Powered by TrueGrade" 
  appears small in the footer.

CORE PHILOSOPHY (READ FIRST):
- NO pre-installed rate data, NO hardcoded production rates, NO seeded price 
  database. The app ships as an empty, guided shell. Every number that feeds 
  an estimate is entered, uploaded, or confirmed by the company using it.
- AI ASSISTS, HUMANS VERIFY. Every AI-extracted quantity, material, or price 
  is a draft until a human confirms it. Nothing flows into an estimate 
  silently.
- EVERYTHING IS TRACEABLE. Every quantity links to the document, sheet, and 
  location it came from. Every price shows its source and date.
- Users are estimators bidding earthwork, utilities, paving, and structures 
  (bridges) on government, state, and commercial jobs. Precision beats speed. 
  If the numbers don't match reality, they will stop using the tool.

MULTI-TENANCY (applies everywhere):
- Every table and every stored file scoped by company_id; enforce at the 
  query layer and storage layer, not just the UI
- Roles: Admin (setup, rates, approvals, supplier directory, email settings) 
  and Estimator (builds estimates, sends and exports RFQs)
- Subdomain routing per company
- No cross-tenant data leakage, ever. Automated tests must attempt 
  cross-tenant reads of records AND uploaded files AND RFQ download links 
  and confirm they fail.

===== PART 1: COMPANY SETUP WIZARD ("Make It Yours") =====

Runs on first login and stays permanently accessible from the main nav as 
"Company Setup." Every step is skippable, saveable mid-way, and re-editable. 
Show a progress indicator and a "setup completeness" score. Any estimate 
built on incomplete setup visibly flags which line items rely on 
unverified values.

1. COMPANY BASICS
   Name, logo, accent color, default markup %, overhead allocation %, sales 
   tax rate, project types bid (Gov/State/Commercial), bonding/insurance 
   cost approach, default RFQ email subject line, message body, and 
   signature.

2. LABOR
   - Roles (operator, laborer, foreman, flagger, pipe layer, etc.) with base 
     wage, burden %, and a separate prevailing wage / Davis-Bacon rate
   - Prevailing wage per county/project via a simple table, or upload the 
     wage determination PDF for AI extraction with review

3. EQUIPMENT
   - Each machine: name, type, hourly ownership cost, hourly operating cost, 
     standby rate, mobilization cost per move
   - Option to upload an equipment cost report for AI extraction with review

4. MATERIALS & SUPPLIERS
   - Materials: name, unit, unit cost, supplier, date last quoted, density 
     (tons/CY) where relevant, waste/overage %, spec notes
   - Quote-expiry warnings for materials not updated within a user-set 
     number of days
   - SUPPLIER DIRECTORY: supplier company, contacts, emails, phones, 
     categories supplied (aggregates, pipe & precast, asphalt, ready-mix 
     concrete, rebar & steel, geotextiles & erosion control, fittings & 
     valves, trucking, equipment rental, plus custom categories), service 
     area, preferred flag, notes. Bulk import from CSV/Excel.
   - Subcontractor directory with the same structure (striping, fencing, 
     electrical, traffic control, etc.)

5. PRODUCTION RATES
   - For each activity, the user enters real-world crew production and picks 
     the equipment and labor roles that make up the crew
   - Ask in plain language ("How many cubic yards can your crew place and 
     compact in one 8-hour day?") and convert internally. The user never 
     needs to know the formula.
   - Rates can be overridden per job later

6. SOIL & MATERIAL BEHAVIOR
   - Editable table of soil types with swell % and shrink %, plain-language 
     explanations, custom soil types, and a before/after volume visual

7. ASSEMBLIES (pre-built work packages)
   - Guided builder: name an assembly (e.g., "8-inch sewer, 8 ft deep"), pick 
     materials (with quantity per unit of assembly), crew, equipment, and 
     production rate from setup data. Live cost per unit with breakdown. 
     Duplicate and edit freely.
   - Assemblies are what convert takeoff quantities into material lists, so 
     material quantity per unit must be explicit (e.g., per LF of pipe: 
     1.05 LF pipe, X CY bedding, X CY backfill).

8. EMAIL SENDING (optional; the app works fully without completing this)
   Offer three ways to send RFQs from inside the app. Explain each in plain 
   language with pros and cons, and let the Admin pick a company default:
   a) TRUEGRADE SENDING (default, works immediately, no setup): emails sent 
      from the platform's authenticated domain, display name 
      "[Company Name] via TrueGrade", reply-to set to the sending estimator.
   b) COMPANY DOMAIN: emails sent from the company's own address (e.g., 
      bids@theircompany.com). Show the exact SPF, DKIM, and DMARC DNS records 
      to add, with copy buttons, a "send these instructions to my IT / web 
      person" button, and a live "Verify" check. Until verified, fall back to 
      option (a) automatically and say so.
   c) CONNECT MY EMAIL: each estimator connects their own Google or Microsoft 
      account via OAuth. RFQs send from their real mailbox and appear in 
      their Sent folder. Respect provider daily sending limits and warn 
      before hitting them.

===== PART 2: PLAN ROOM & TAKEOFF ("Upload the Bid Package") =====

A. UPLOAD
- Each estimate has a Bid Package. Users upload: plan set (PDF, often 
  20-500+ sheets and 50-500 MB), bid schedule / bid form (PDF or Excel), 
  specifications and special provisions, geotechnical report, addenda, and 
  optional CAD exchange files (LandXML surfaces, DXF/DWG stored for 
  reference, CSV point files).
- Chunked, resumable uploads. Processing runs as background jobs with a 
  progress indicator; the user can keep working while it processes.
- Auto-suggest document type tags; user confirms.
- Capture: owner, owner type (Federal / State DOT / County-City / Private), 
  project number, location, bid due date and time, pre-bid meeting.

B. SHEET INDEX
- Split the plan set into sheets. Extract title block data (sheet number, 
  title, discipline) into a searchable index with thumbnails.
- Classify sheets: cover, general notes, quantity summary, grading, utility 
  plan & profile, paving, structures, details, traffic control, erosion 
  control, cross sections. User can correct classifications.
- Detect vector vs. scanned PDFs. Use embedded text and vector data where 
  available; use OCR/vision only when needed.
- Run heavy AI vision only on sheets relevant to takeoff. Before full 
  analysis, show estimated processing time and cost. Admin sets per-tenant 
  processing limits.

C. QUANTITY EXTRACTION (three sources, ranked by reliability; every quantity 
   records its source)
1. BID SCHEDULE (most reliable): Extract the owner's bid item schedule into 
   an editable table: item number, description, spec section reference, 
   unit, quantity. When a bid schedule exists, the estimate is organized by 
   these bid items so the final bid maps 1:1 to the owner's bid form.
2. TABULATED DATA ON PLANS: Extract quantity summary sheets, pipe schedules, 
   structure/manhole tables, curb/sidewalk tables, and earthwork summary 
   tables, each with its sheet reference.
3. AI-ASSISTED MEASUREMENT (least reliable, always flagged amber): Suggest 
   pipe run lengths by size and material from plan & profile sheets, counts 
   of structures (manholes, inlets, valves, hydrants), and paved areas by 
   section type. Show suggestions as overlays on the sheet with 
   Accept / Edit / Reject.
- EARTHWORK VOLUMES: Do NOT compute cut/fill from PDF contours with AI. 
  Accept earthwork quantities only from (a) the owner's bid schedule or 
  earthwork summary, (b) imported LandXML existing and proposed surfaces, 
  compared to compute cut/fill, or (c) manual entry from an external 
  earthwork takeoff, with a source note. Apply the company's swell/shrink 
  factors from Setup.

D. TAKEOFF WORKSPACE (manual tools, always available)
- Plan viewer: zoom, pan, sheet navigation, search by sheet number or title
- Scale: detect from title block, but require the user to calibrate each 
  sheet by clicking two points of known distance before measuring. Warn if 
  the PDF appears printed at a different size than the stated scale.
- Tools: linear, polyline, area, and count, with color-coded markups mapped 
  to a bid item or assembly
- Every takeoff quantity links to its markup. Clicking a quantity opens the 
  sheet at that exact location.
- If the company's own takeoff differs from the owner's bid schedule 
  quantity by more than a user-set %, flag it for review.

E. MANDATORY REVIEW
- One review table with every extracted or suggested quantity: source, 
  confidence (amber = low), and sheet link. Each must be confirmed or edited 
  before it can feed the estimate.

F. ADDENDA
- Upload addenda anytime. Detect changed bid items, quantities, and sheets, 
  show a clear before/after diff, and let the user accept changes into the 
  estimate. Keep version history of the bid package. Track which addenda 
  have been acknowledged for the bid form.
- If an addendum changes quantities on RFQs already sent or exported, flag 
  the affected RFQs and offer to generate revised versions (labeled 
  "Revision 1", "Revision 2", etc.).

G. SPECIFICATIONS
- Extract material requirements referenced by bid items (aggregate type and 
  gradation, asphalt binder grade, pipe material and class, concrete 
  strength, etc.) with spec section references, so material lists and RFQs 
  state exactly what is required.
- If a bid item references standard specifications that weren't uploaded 
  (e.g., state DOT standard specs), flag it and let the user upload them or 
  enter the requirement manually.

===== PART 3: HISTORICAL JOB IMPORT ("Teach TrueGrade From Your Past Jobs") =====

1. UPLOAD past job cost reports: PDF, Excel/CSV, images of printed reports. 
   Multiple files per job (bid estimate + final job cost report).
2. AI EXTRACTION of line items (activity, quantity, unit, estimated cost, 
   actual cost, hours, equipment, materials).
3. MANDATORY REVIEW beside the source document; low-confidence fields in 
   amber; never auto-import.
4. MAPPING to existing setup, with inline "create new" for unmatched items.
5. VARIANCE CALIBRATION: estimated vs. actual per activity with suggested 
   adjustments, each requiring Approve / Edit / Dismiss. Never auto-adjust.
6. Manual entry form for companies without clean documents.

===== PART 4: ESTIMATE BUILDER (per job) =====

- Pre-populated from confirmed takeoff quantities. When a bid schedule 
  exists, each bid item is a section; otherwise use the default sections: 
  Project Setup → Earthwork → Wet Utilities → Paving & Concrete → Structures 
  → Indirect Costs.
- BID ITEM MAPPING: Each bid item is mapped to one or more assemblies. AI 
  suggests mappings; the user confirms. Unmapped bid items are flagged and 
  block final output.
- Project Setup: type, labor mode (open shop vs. prevailing wage), haul 
  distances, tax, permits
- JOB-LEVEL OVERRIDES: Any rate, production rate, material price, or 
  equipment cost can be overridden for this job without changing company 
  defaults. Overrides are visibly marked and logged with an optional reason.
- FORMULA TRANSPARENCY: Every line has a "How was this calculated?" expander 
  showing the math in plain English and which setup values and quantities 
  fed it (with links to the source sheet).
- CONFIDENCE INDICATOR: Each line shows whether it's based on calibrated 
  data, verified company values, supplier quotes, or unverified values. A 
  summary reads, for example: "82% of this estimate is based on calibrated, 
  verified, or quoted data."
- Saveable, duplicable, versioned.

===== PART 5: MATERIAL LIST & SUPPLIER RFQs ("Get Prices") =====

A. MATERIAL LIST
- Generate from confirmed quantities × assembly material components: every 
  material with quantity, unit, spec requirement and section, waste %, and 
  unit conversions using the company's own values (CY to tons via density, 
  SY × thickness to tons, swell/shrink for import and export).
- Consolidate identical materials across bid items while keeping 
  traceability to which bid items use them.
- Fully editable before sending; users can add or remove lines.
- Build subcontractor scope packages the same way (scope description, 
  related bid items, quantities).

B. RFQ GENERATION
- Generate one RFQ per supplier category, plus a combined master RFQ.
- Every RFQ gets a unique RFQ number (e.g., IR-2026-0142-AGG-R0: company 
  prefix, job number, category, revision), shown in the file name, the 
  spreadsheet header, and the email subject, so any returned file can be 
  matched to the right job, category, and revision.
- Header: company logo, RFQ number, project name, owner, project number, 
  location, bid date, quote due date/time, delivery location, estimator 
  name/phone/email, addenda included, and a "SUPPLIER NAME / CONTACT" 
  field for the supplier to fill in.
- Line columns: hidden line ID (for re-import matching), description, spec 
  requirement and section, quantity, unit, UNIT PRICE (blank), EXTENDED 
  PRICE (formula), lead time (blank), notes / alternate offered (blank).
- Terms block (blank for supplier): quote valid until, tax included Y/N, 
  freight/delivery included Y/N, minimum order, exclusions.
- Lock all cells except supplier-entry cells.
- Two versions of each RFQ:
  - GENERIC: supplier name left blank, for sending to anyone
  - PER-SUPPLIER: supplier name and contact pre-filled from the directory, 
    so the return is automatically attributed to that supplier

C. TWO WAYS TO GET RFQs OUT (both are first-class; neither is a fallback)

C1. SEND FROM TRUEGRADE
- Select suppliers per category from the directory (suggested by category 
  and service area). The estimator previews and approves every email before 
  it is sent. Uses the company's chosen sending method (Setup step 8).
- Each email contains the .xlsx attachment AND a secure no-login link to an 
  online quote form with the same lines.
- Status per supplier: sent, opened, responded, declined, overdue. 
  Automatic reminders to suppliers at user-set intervals before the due 
  time. Full activity log.

C2. EXPORT AND SEND IT MYSELF
- On every RFQ, a prominent "Download" button offering:
  - Excel (.xlsx), the recommended format for suppliers to fill in
  - PDF (read-only, for reference or suppliers who prefer to write their 
    own quote)
  - CSV
  - Per-supplier copies (one pre-filled file per selected supplier)
  - "Download all" as a single .zip containing every category RFQ for the 
    job, named clearly (e.g., "IR-2026-0142 - Aggregates - R0.xlsx")
- "Copy email text" button: copies a ready-to-paste subject line and 
  message body (from the company's default template in Setup step 1) with 
  the RFQ number, project name, and quote due date filled in.
- "Open in my email" button: opens the user's default email program with 
  subject and body pre-filled (mailto). Note in the UI that the file must 
  be attached manually, since mailto cannot attach files.
- Allow downloading and forwarding to anyone, including people outside the 
  supplier directory (internal team members, partners, new suppliers).
- After a manual send, "Mark as sent" lets the user record who it went to 
  (pick from the directory or type a name/email; unknown recipients can be 
  saved to the directory in one click) and when. Manually sent RFQs then 
  appear in the same status tracker, labeled "Sent outside TrueGrade." 
  Open tracking is unavailable for these; say so plainly.
- Reminders for manually sent RFQs go to the ESTIMATOR ("Aggregates quote 
  from [supplier] is due in 24 hours and hasn't been logged"), not the 
  supplier.
- Log every download (who, when, which file, which revision).
- If an RFQ is downloaded and the estimate's quantities change afterward, 
  show a banner: "This RFQ changed after you downloaded it. Download 
  Revision 1?"

D. QUOTE RETURN & IMPORT (works the same no matter how the RFQ was sent)
- Accept prices four ways:
  1. Online quote form (direct entry by supplier)
  2. Filled .xlsx upload: drag and drop; match to the job and category by 
     RFQ number and to lines by hidden line IDs
  3. The supplier's own PDF or document quote: AI extraction, then 
     mandatory review and mapping to material lines
  4. Forwarded or pasted email text: AI extraction with mandatory review
- If a returned spreadsheet has been reformatted and line IDs are missing, 
  fall back to AI matching by description, quantity, and unit, with 
  mandatory review.
- If the returned file is a generic RFQ, ask which supplier it came from 
  (pre-select if the supplier filled in their name).
- Capture exclusions, alternates, validity date, and freight terms.
- Bulk upload: several returned quotes at once, each auto-matched and 
  queued for review.

E. QUOTE COMPARISON
- Side-by-side view per material line across suppliers. Highlight lowest 
  complete price; flag missing lines, exclusions, expired quotes, and 
  alternates that may not meet spec. Flag quotes priced against an 
  outdated RFQ revision.
- Estimator selects a quote per line or per supplier. Selected prices 
  populate the estimate, labeled with supplier, quote date, and expiry.
- With approval, selected quotes update the company's material price history.
- Lines with no quote fall back to the company's last known price, flagged 
  "unquoted."
- Export the comparison to Excel.

===== PART 6: POST-JOB CLOSE-OUT =====
- "Close out this job": enter or upload final actual costs against the saved 
  estimate, feeding the same review and calibration process as Part 3.

===== PART 7: OUTPUT =====
- Bid summary PDF with company branding: line items, subtotals, markup, 
  total. "Client version" (hides internal cost detail) and "internal 
  version."
- Bid form export: unit prices and extensions in the exact item order of 
  the owner's bid schedule, as Excel and PDF, for transfer to the official 
  bid form.
- Full estimate export to Excel.

===== UX RULES (NON-NEGOTIABLE) =====
- Plain language everywhere; every technical term has a hover/tap explainer.
- Nothing is locked. Setup values are editable from one place and flow to 
  future estimates. Saved estimates keep original values unless the user 
  explicitly recalculates.
- Helpful empty states: every empty screen explains what belongs there and 
  offers "add manually," "upload documents," or "skip for now."
- Autosave everywhere.
- Calm palette. Amber for warnings and flags. Red only for destructive 
  delete confirmations.
- Desktop-first (plan viewer needs screen space); forms usable on a tablet.
- Every AI-extracted or AI-suggested value is labeled as such until a human 
  confirms it.
- Every quantity and price shows its source.
- Every file the app generates is downloadable. The user is never forced to 
  send, share, or finish anything inside TrueGrade.
